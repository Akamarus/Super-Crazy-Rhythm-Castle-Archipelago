# AI disclosure

I’m Akamarus, the maintainer of this Super Crazy Rhythm Castle integration. I decide the project’s direction, which features to build, how the randomizer should work, and what is ready to release. That includes progression rules, item and location behavior, access requirements, options, and the player experience.

OpenAI Codex has been used extensively to write the Python APWorld and C# client, investigate bugs, analyze code, create automated tests, and help with documentation and release notes. Substantial portions of the implementation and fixes are AI-written; this goes well beyond autocomplete.

My involvement is ongoing and hands-on. Over weeks of development and many testing sessions, I have played through randomized seeds, completed runs, checked rewards and progression against the actual game, and repeatedly retested fixes. I provide screenshots, logs, and movement recordings; identify incorrect assumptions; change designs when they do not work in practice; and report regressions for further investigation. The project has involved a sustained cycle of my decisions, testing, feedback, and corrections—not simply accepting an AI’s first result and posting it.

My human review is strongest in design, game behavior, and gameplay validation. I am not claiming comprehensive human source-code review or that every feature, seed, and configuration has been tested. Automated tests and AI reviews do not replace that, and the project documents known issues and testing limits. I remain responsible for what I publish.

I welcome a developer who wants to audit the code, review the implementation, or help maintain it. If a developer is interested in taking over the project, I am happy to discuss that and cooperate with a handover, including sharing the project’s history, design decisions, testing evidence, and outstanding issues.
