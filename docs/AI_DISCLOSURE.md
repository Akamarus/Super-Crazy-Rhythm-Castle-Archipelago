# AI disclosure

I'm posting this to be open about how this project is developed and in line with Archipelago's new AI Disclosure Policy.

I'm Akamarus, the maintainer of this Super Crazy Rhythm Castle integration. I decide its direction, features, randomizer behavior, and what is ready to release. That includes progression rules, items and locations, access requirements, options, and the player experience.

OpenAI Codex has been used extensively to write the Python APWorld and C# client, investigate bugs, analyze code, create automated tests, and help with documentation and release notes. Substantial portions of the implementation and fixes are AI-written; this goes well beyond autocomplete.

My involvement is ongoing and hands-on. Over weeks of development and many testing sessions, I have played randomized seeds, completed runs, checked rewards and progression against the actual game, and repeatedly retested fixes. I provide screenshots, logs, and movement recordings; challenge incorrect assumptions; revise designs; and report regressions. This is a sustained cycle of my decisions, testing, feedback, and corrections—not simply accepting an AI's first result and posting it.

I also want to be clear about the limits: my human review is strongest in design, game behavior, and gameplay validation. I am not claiming comprehensive human source-code review or that every feature, seed, and configuration has been tested. Automated tests and AI reviews do not replace that. Known issues and testing limits are documented, and I remain responsible for what I publish.

I intend to keep leading and improving this project. I welcome code audits, contributions, and cooperative development or co-maintenance. If a developer has concerns or believes they could better maintain it, I'm open to discussing those and working together. I would consider a handover if it would better serve the project and its players, and would cooperate fully in that transition.
